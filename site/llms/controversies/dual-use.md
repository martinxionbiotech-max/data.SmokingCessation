# Dual Use: Smoking + Vaping

Using e-cigarettes while still smoking cigarettes. Positioned by some as a transition stage; by others as a trap.

## Evidence supporting dual use as a bridge

- In observational data, some dual users later transition to exclusive vaping or quit entirely; vaping reduces cigarette consumption for some.
- Harm-reduction logic: fewer cigarettes smoked means lower (not zero) toxicant exposure, and NRT harm-reduction precedents exist.

## Evidence against / limitations

- **Most dual users remain dual users** — in several cohort studies the majority do not quit smoking within follow-up.
- Continued smoking keeps most smoking-related risk; harm reduction requires actually stopping cigarettes.
- Nicotine intake can be higher during dual use than while smoking alone, prolonging dependence.
- WHO cautions that dual use can sustain smoking rather than end it.

## What is known vs uncertain

- **Known**: dual use reduces harm only insofar as it reduces cigarette consumption; it does not eliminate smoking risk; many dual users do not transition.
- **Uncertain**: how often dual use is a genuine stepping stone versus a lasting state; long-term outcomes.

## Platform treatment

Community records that describe dual use are reported as experiences. The platform's position: **the goal is stopping combustible cigarettes**; dual use is at best an unstable intermediate state, and anyone dual-using is encouraged to set a date to stop cigarettes completely.
