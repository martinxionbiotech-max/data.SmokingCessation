# Fatigue — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

Unusual tiredness, low energy or heavy sleepiness in early withdrawal.

## Why it happens

Nicotine is a stimulant; withdrawal removes a chronic activation source. Sleep disruption, stress and the energy cost of brain re-adaptation all contribute.

## Typical timing

Common in the first 1–2 weeks, improving over 2–4 weeks.

## What research says

Fatigue is documented in withdrawal symptom research, though it overlaps with sleep and mood symptoms (see R-007).

## What varies between people

Some people feel energized immediately; others feel drained. People quitting with anemia, thyroid conditions or poor sleep report more fatigue.

## What may help

Prioritizing sleep, short walks (mild activity paradoxically reduces fatigue), regular meals, and temporarily reducing commitments in the first weeks.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

Fatigue persisting well beyond the withdrawal window, or accompanied by breathlessness, pallor, weight loss or night sweats, needs medical review.
