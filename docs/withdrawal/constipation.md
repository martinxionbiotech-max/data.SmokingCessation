# Constipation — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

Less frequent or harder bowel movements in the first weeks after quitting.

## Why it happens

Nicotine stimulates bowel motility; its removal slows the gut temporarily. This is the inverse of the appetite increase — both reflect the gut adjusting to life without nicotine.

## Typical timing

Typically appears in week 1–2 and resolves within 2–4 weeks.

## What research says

Constipation is a recognized but less-studied withdrawal symptom (see R-007); gut effects are well known clinically.

## What varies between people

People with pre-existing slow gut habits or low fiber intake notice it most; many people never experience it.

## What may help

Water, dietary fiber, physical movement, and not ignoring the urge. Temporary use of standard fiber supplements or mild laxatives as directed is common practice.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

Constipation with severe pain, blood in stool, or lasting more than a few weeks warrants medical evaluation.
