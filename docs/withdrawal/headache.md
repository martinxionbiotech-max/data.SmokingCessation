# Headache — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

Headaches in the first weeks after quitting, ranging from mild tension-type to more noticeable episodes.

## Why it happens

Multiple mechanisms: blood flow changes as nicotine's vascular effects withdraw, caffeine-nicotine interaction changes, sleep disruption, and general stress. Nicotine is a vasoconstrictor; withdrawal changes cerebral circulation.

## Typical timing

Usually within the first days to week, resolving over 1–3 weeks.

## What research says

Headache is a documented though less consistently reported withdrawal symptom (see R-007).

## What varies between people

Caffeine users who change coffee intake while quitting often trigger headaches — nicotine speeds caffeine metabolism, so the same coffee is effectively stronger after quitting.

## What may help

Regular hydration, stable sleep, watching caffeine intake (consider slightly reducing coffee in the first weeks), and standard over-the-counter pain relief used as directed.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

Sudden severe headache, headache with visual changes, weakness, confusion or stiff neck needs urgent medical evaluation — these are not withdrawal.
