# Anxiety — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

Feelings of tension, worry, nervousness or panic-like symptoms during the withdrawal period.

## Why it happens

Nicotine withdrawal dysregulates the stress-response systems that nicotine chronically activated; quitting also removes a coping behaviour people often used to manage stress, temporarily unmasking anxiety.

## Typical timing

Typically emerges in the first days, peaks in week 1, and settles over 2–4 weeks for most people.

## What research says

Anxiety is documented as a withdrawal symptom (see R-007), though the effect size varies across studies. People with anxiety disorders may notice a more pronounced response early in quitting.

## What varies between people

Wide variation: many people report calmness and relief rather than anxiety; others report notable tension. Baseline anxiety matters — someone quitting with an untreated anxiety disorder may need extra support.

## What may help

Slow breathing exercises, reducing caffeine, physical activity, structured relaxation, and planned responses to worry spirals. Behavioural support can help distinguish withdrawal anxiety from underlying anxiety.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

Seek professional help for panic attacks, persistent severe anxiety lasting beyond the withdrawal window, or if anxiety prevents normal functioning.
