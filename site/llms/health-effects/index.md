# Health Effects: What Smoking Does and What Quitting Changes

The evidence layer on harm and recovery. The body-recovery timeline on the [main site](https://smokingcessationhub.com/timeline/) is the practical version of this page.

## The core evidence

- Smoking is causally linked to cancer, cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease, and many other conditions — the causal framework is documented across Surgeon General reports and WHO reports spanning decades.
- The landmark Jha et al. study of ~200,000 US adults found: quitting before age 40 avoids most of the excess mortality of continued smoking; quitting between 25–34 adds roughly a decade of life; quitting at any age confers benefit — see [R-006](../research/records/r-006.md).

## What happens after quitting (documented averages)

| Timeframe | Documented change |
|---|---|
| 20 min | Heart rate and blood pressure begin to drop |
| 12–24 h | Carbon monoxide clears from blood |
| Weeks | Circulation and lung function improve; coughing may transiently increase as cilia recover |
| 1–9 months | Cilia function recovers; cough and shortness of breath typically decrease |
| 1 year | Excess coronary heart disease risk roughly halves |
| 5 years | Stroke risk approaches that of a non-smoker |
| 10+ years | Lung cancer risk falls substantially; risks keep declining |

*These are population averages summarized from US Surgeon General and WHO materials; individual outcomes vary.*

## What the community reports

Community experiences in the database frequently describe easier breathing within weeks, taste and smell returning within days, and energy improvements over months. These reports are **consistent with** the evidence — they are not themselves evidence, and individual absence of noticeable benefit does not mean absence of biological recovery.

## What does NOT happen

- No health effect appears instantly reversible at the moment of the last cigarette.
- Quitting does not erase all accumulated risk; it substantially reduces future additional risk.
- Harm reduction products (e-cigarettes) are covered under [Controversies](../controversies/) — long-term safety is genuinely uncertain.
