# Cessation Medications

Evidence pages for the medications with the strongest published evidence: nicotine replacement therapy (NRT), varenicline, bupropion and cytisine. Each page reports what the medication is, how it works, what trials and systematic reviews show, common considerations, and questions to ask a healthcare professional.

**This is medication information, not medical advice and not prescriptions.** Dosing, suitability and interactions are individual clinical decisions. Any medication can have side effects; none of these pages substitutes for discussion with a prescriber or pharmacist.

## Evidence strength overview

| Medication | Best evidence | Effect on quit rates vs placebo (approx.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Varenicline | Cochrane review of partial agonists | Roughly 2.2–2.9x | Also supported by EAGLES safety trial |
| NRT (any form) | Cochrane NRT review | Roughly 1.5–1.7x | Many forms; combination of patch + fast-acting form is common |
| Bupropion | Cochrane antidepressant review | Roughly 1.6x | Not with seizure-risk conditions |
| Cytisine | Cochrane + NEJM trials | Roughly 1.7–3x vs placebo; similar to NRT in comparative trials | Very low cost; not available in all countries |

*Figures are approximate ranges from the systematic reviews listed; exact estimates depend on setting and outcome definition. See the Research Database records for precise findings.*
