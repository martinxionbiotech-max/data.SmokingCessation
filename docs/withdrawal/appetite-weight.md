# Increased Appetite and Weight Change — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

Greater hunger, snacking, taste improvement and weight gain after quitting.

## Why it happens

Nicotine suppresses appetite and slightly raises metabolism; withdrawal reverses both. Food also substitutes for the oral habit, and taste/smell recovery makes food more rewarding. Weight gain is typically modest.

## Typical timing

Appetite rises in the first weeks; weight gain typically occurs over the first months, with most of it in the first 3–6 months.

## What research says

Average weight gain after quitting is commonly cited as about 4–5 kg over the first year in population studies, with wide variation — many people gain less, some more. Increased appetite is documented in R-007.

## What varies between people

Highly variable: some people gain no weight; heavy smokers gain more on average. Fear of weight gain is a documented barrier to quitting for some groups.

## What may help

Planning snacks (fruit, vegetables, water), keeping hands busy, building in physical activity from day one, and accepting modest weight change as vastly less harmful than continued smoking.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

Rapid large weight gain or disordered eating patterns deserve professional attention; also note bupropion is sometimes discussed in this context — talk to a clinician.
