# Irritability — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

Lower frustration tolerance, short temper, anger and general edginess in the first weeks after quitting.

## Why it happens

The nicotine-adapted brain temporarily loses the calming effect of repeated nicotine dosing; combined with sleep disruption and the stress of breaking a habit, frustration tolerance drops.

## Typical timing

Appears within 1–3 days, peaks in the first week, and typically resolves within 2–4 weeks.

## What research says

Irritability/anger is one of the most consistently documented withdrawal symptoms in the literature (see R-007). It is rated among the most severe symptoms by people quitting.

## What varies between people

Severity ranges from mild edginess to notable anger episodes. People with pre-existing stress or mood conditions may experience more pronounced irritability.

## What may help

Warning family and colleagues, scheduled physical activity, adequate sleep, reducing caffeine late in the day, and giving yourself permission to step away from tense situations.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

If anger becomes uncontrollable, or is accompanied by severe mood disturbance or thoughts of self-harm, seek professional help — that is beyond typical withdrawal.
