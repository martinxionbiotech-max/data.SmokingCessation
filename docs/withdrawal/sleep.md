# Sleep Problems — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

Insomnia, frequent waking, vivid dreams or daytime sleepiness in the weeks after quitting.

## Why it happens

Nicotine is a stimulant and its withdrawal changes sleep architecture; nicotine also suppresses REM sleep, and quitting produces a rebound of vivid dreaming as REM normalizes.

## Typical timing

Sleep disturbance typically starts in the first days, peaks in week 1, and improves over 2–4 weeks. Vivid dreams can persist longer, especially with 24-hour nicotine patch use.

## What research says

Sleep disturbance and increased dreaming are documented withdrawal features (see R-007). Vivid dreams are also a known effect of nicotine patches worn overnight.

## What varies between people

Some people sleep better almost immediately (no more nocturnal nicotine cravings); others struggle for weeks. Patch users who sleep poorly often benefit from removing the patch at night.

## What may help

Consistent sleep schedule, reducing caffeine after midday, evening wind-down routines, and avoiding screens before bed. If using a 24-hour patch and dreams are disruptive, discuss patch timing with a pharmacist.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

Prolonged severe insomnia beyond the withdrawal window, or sleep apnea symptoms (loud snoring with pauses, daytime sleepiness), warrant medical review.
