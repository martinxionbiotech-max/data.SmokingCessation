# E-Cigarettes for Smoking Cessation

The single largest controversy in cessation. The evidence is strong in one direction and the guidelines genuinely split.

## Evidence supporting e-cigarettes as cessation aids

- **Cochrane living review (2024 update, R-013)**: high-certainty evidence that nicotine e-cigarettes increase quit rates compared with NRT; moderate-certainty evidence versus behavioural support or no support. This upgraded earlier (moderate) certainty ratings.
- **Hajek et al., NEJM 2019 (R-005)**: in a UK stop-smoking service, e-cigarette users achieved roughly double the 1-year abstinence of NRT users (18.0% vs 9.9%).
- **NICE NG209**: clinicians may offer nicotine-containing e-cigarettes to people interested in using them to quit, with advice to stop smoking completely.

## Evidence against / limitations

- **Long-term safety is unknown.** E-cigarettes are a relatively new product class; decades-long outcomes do not exist. Non-nicotine constituents (flavourings, aerosol byproducts) are under active study.
- **WHO (2024)**: does not recommend e-cigarettes as cessation aids, citing insufficient evidence of safety and effectiveness at population level; notes industry involvement in the market.
- **USPSTF (2021)**: I statement — evidence insufficient to assess the balance of benefits and harms; directs clinicians to interventions with proven effectiveness.
- **Youth uptake concern**: non-smoking young people who vape are at higher risk of later cigarette smoking in observational studies — a public-health harm independent of any cessation benefit.
- **Dual use** is common and its net effect is debated (see [dual-use](dual-use.md)).

## Guideline positions (as published)

| Body | Position |
|---|---|
| Cochrane (evidence body) | High-certainty evidence of efficacy vs NRT |
| NICE NG209 (UK) | May be offered as a cessation aid |
| WHO 2024 | Not recommended as cessation aid |
| USPSTF 2021 (US) | Evidence insufficient (I) |

## What is known vs uncertain

- **Known**: nicotine e-cigarettes outperform NRT in randomized trials for quitting; they are not harmless; they are inappropriate for non-smokers, especially youth.
- **Uncertain**: long-term health effects; net population impact; optimal role relative to approved medications.

## Platform position

The platform treats e-cigarettes as **experimental-adjacent but trial-supported** — stronger than "unproven", weaker than approved medications, and never for non-smokers. Community reports of vaping-assisted quits are recorded in the experience database without endorsement.
