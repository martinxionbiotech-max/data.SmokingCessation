# Restlessness — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

An inability to sit still, fidgeting, pacing, or a constant sense of needing to do something with the hands.

## Why it happens

Nicotine withdrawal produces psychomotor agitation; the smoking ritual also occupied the hands and filled micro-breaks, and removing it leaves a physical void.

## Typical timing

Early and prominent in the first week, fading over 2–4 weeks.

## What research says

Restlessness is among the consistently measured withdrawal symptoms (see R-007).

## What varies between people

People with strong hand-mouth rituals (smoking while working, driving, phoning) report more restlessness. Activity level also matters — moving people are less bothered.

## What may help

Fidget substitutes (pens, stress balls), short walks, standing breaks, chewing sugar-free gum, and keeping hands busy during formerly-smoking activities.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

Not usually a medical concern; if accompanied by chest pain, palpitations or tremor, seek evaluation.
