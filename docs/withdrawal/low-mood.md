# Low Mood — Withdrawal Science

## Definition

Sadness, low energy of mood, reduced interest or mild depressive feelings during withdrawal.

## Why it happens

Removing nicotine disrupts dopamine signalling that cigarettes chronically stimulated; the brain needs time to re-regulate. Loss of a daily ritual and its social rewards also contributes.

## Typical timing

Usually begins in the first days, is most noticeable in weeks 1–2, and improves by ~4 weeks.

## What research says

Depressed mood is a documented withdrawal symptom (see R-007). It is distinct from clinical depression: withdrawal low mood is time-limited and resolves as neuroadaptation reverses.

## What varies between people

People with a history of depression are at higher risk of a more pronounced low mood, and it can be hard to tell withdrawal low mood from emerging depressive relapse — an important reason to involve a professional early.

## What may help

Maintaining routines, physical activity (with the strongest evidence for mood), social contact, realistic expectations about the first weeks, and planning pleasant activities deliberately.

## When medical advice may be appropriate

If low mood persists beyond a month, deepens, or includes hopelessness or thoughts of self-harm, seek professional evaluation promptly. This is a safety boundary, not a weakness.
